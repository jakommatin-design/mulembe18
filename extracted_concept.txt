mulembe18: National Community Platform
Proposal
mulembe18 will grow from a Mombasa pilot into a chapter-based national platform that
registers Luhya community members across Kenya, coordinates community events, and
supports welfare and contributions in a transparent way.
1. Executive Summary
mulembe18 is a web platform that gives Luhya people in Kenya one trusted place to
register, find each other, plan events and support one another. The first chapter will serve
Mombasa. The design is then repeated county by county, so each chapter runs its own
members, events and funds while sharing one national identity and one national
leadership.
This document sets out the purpose, structure, features, technical approach, governance,
risks and rollout needed to take the platform from a single city to the whole country.
2. Background and Rationale
Many members of the community live away from their home counties for work and study.
They often meet through informal WhatsApp groups, church networks and word of
mouth. These channels work, but they leave gaps:
There is no reliable record of who lives where, so newcomers struggle to find the
community.
Event planning is scattered across chat groups, with no central calendar, headcount or
task tracking.
Welfare contributions, such as bereavement and emergency support, are collected
informally, which makes it hard to account for the money.
Each city's group works alone, so the wider community cannot organize national
events or share resources.
A structured platform addresses these gaps while keeping the personal, community feel
that people value. Comparable models include county and diaspora associations, alumni
directories, chama management apps and event platforms.

3. Vision, Mission and Objectives
Vision: a connected, supportive Luhya community across Kenya, where no member is a
stranger in a new town.
Mission: to provide a secure, transparent and easy-to-use platform that brings members
together, organizes community events, and supports welfare and development.
Objectives
1. Register and verify members, organized by chapter, home county and sub-community.
2. Give every chapter tools to plan events and track attendance.
3. Make welfare and dues collection transparent, with a public record of money received
and spent.
4. Provide a national layer for leadership, announcements and community-wide events.
5. Protect members' personal data and comply with Kenyan law.
6. Keep the platform accessible to members with low data and basic phones.
4. Organizational Structure
The platform is built around chapters. Each member belongs to the chapter where they
live, and each chapter manages its own affairs under national rules.

5. Membership and Directory
Every member has a profile that is verified by a chapter admin before it becomes active.
Profile fields: full name, phone number, email, sub-community or clan, home county,
current chapter and area, occupation or skills, and student or professional status.
Privacy controls: members decide which fields other members can see. Chapter
admins see what they need to manage the chapter. National officials see aggregate
data unless a member consents to more.
Search and filters: by chapter, home county, sub-community, skills and interest group,
for example "nurses in Nakuru" or "students in Mombasa."
Verification: admin approval, with optional referral by an existing member to protect
the community from fake sign-ups.
Transfers: a member who relocates can move between chapters without losing their
history.
Exports: admins can export records for official use, and every export is logged.
6. Core Features
6.1 Events and Planning
A national calendar combined with chapter calendars, plus a "near me" view.
Event types: cultural days, weddings, funerals and fundraisers, sports days, church and
youth gatherings, monthly meetups and annual conventions.
RSVP, headcount and reminders by SMS, email and WhatsApp link.
Event committees with named roles (chair, treasurer, logistics) and a task checklist for
each event.

6.2 Welfare, Dues and Contributions
Membership dues tracked per member, with payment reminders.
Bereavement and emergency appeals with a target, a deadline and a running total.
A ledger for each chapter and for the national fund showing money in, money out and
the reason for each payment.
Separate M-Pesa accounts per chapter, so chapter money never mixes with national
money.
Approval rules for payouts, for example two officials must approve any withdrawal.
6.3 Communication
Announcements and a news board at national, chapter and group level.
SMS broadcasts to a chapter or group, so members without smartphones are included.
A noticeboard for jobs, rentals, businesses and mentorship, which helps newcomers
settle into a new town.
6.4 Culture and Identity
Pages on the Luhya sub-communities, language, food, songs and dances.
A photo and video gallery from past events.
A newcomer's guide for each chapter city, written by local members.
6.5 Leadership and Administration
Role-based access: super admin, national officials, chapter admins and group leaders.
Dashboards for member counts, event attendance, dues collected and chapter
activity.
Elections module for chapter and national leadership, with secure voting.
Audit logs that record changes to members, roles and finances.
7. Platform Pages
Home, About, Join, Member Directory (login required), Chapters, Events, Contributions
and Welfare, News, Gallery, Leadership, Newcomer's Guide, Contact and Privacy Policy.

9.3 Core Data Model
9.4 Security and Reliability Design
MySQL and TiDB do not offer PostgreSQL-style row-level security, so chapter isolation
is enforced in the application: every table that holds chapter data carries a chapter ID,
and one shared data-access layer filters every query by the signed-in user’s chapter.
This keeps a coding mistake in one screen from exposing another chapter's data.
Roles and permissions are checked on the server for every request, never only in the
interface, and automated tests confirm that one chapter cannot read another’s data.

M-Pesa callbacks are verified and processed once only, so a repeated callback cannot
record a payment twice.
Payouts require two approvals, and both approvers are recorded in the ledger.
Secrets such as API keys are stored outside the code, and sign-in, sessions and two-
factor checks are handled by Clerk.
Daily database backups are kept in a separate location, and a restore is tested
regularly.
9.5 Deployment and Scaling
1. Launch: a managed host for the web app and a TiDB Cloud database, with automatic
backups and a staging copy for testing.
2. Growth: add background workers for messaging, a content delivery network for
images and extra TiDB capacity as data grows.
3. National scale: separate the messaging and payment services if traffic requires it, and
add stronger monitoring and a documented recovery plan.
10. Legal, Governance and Data Protection
Data protection: the platform will hold personal data of many people. Under the Kenya
Data Protection Act, 2019, the organization will most likely need to register as a data
controller with the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner. Members must give
clear consent at sign-up, and a privacy policy must explain what is collected and why.
Legal identity: register the organization, for example as a society or community-based
organization, so it can open bank accounts, hold funds and sign contracts. Confirm the
right registration route with a lawyer before collecting national funds.
Constitution: a written constitution that defines membership, how chapters are
formed, how leaders are elected, how disputes are resolved and how money is
controlled.
Financial controls: separate accounts for each chapter and for the national fund, dual
approval of payouts, and an annual report to members.
Neutrality: the platform stays non-partisan and does not host political campaigning.
Inclusivity: every sub-community is represented fairly in leadership and content.

5. Build the Phase 1 pilot for the Mombasa chapter.
6. Collect feedback from the pilot and refine before opening new chapters.
