Mulembe18 Community Website Proposal
Prepared by Elisha Adera (@crackertum) | October 2026 | Version 1.0
1. Summary
Mulembe18 is a community website for members who live in Mombasa, Kenya. It has two
jobs: to keep an accurate, living register of community members, and to make it easy to
plan and attend events together. The goal is simple: people who share a background and a
city should know each other, hear about gatherings in time, and show up.
This document is the plan I will follow. It covers why the site is needed, what it will do, how
it will be run, how it will be built, and the order in which things will happen. Items marked
in [square brackets] are decisions or facts I still need to fill in.
2. Background and the problem
Community members living in Mombasa are spread across many estates, workplaces,
colleges and churches. Today, finding each other and organising anything usually depends
on WhatsApp groups, phone calls and word of mouth. That creates real problems:
Nobody has a complete list of who lives in Mombasa, so people are missed.
Announcements get buried in busy group chats.
New arrivals (students, workers, families) have no obvious way to connect.
Event organisers cannot tell how many people to expect, and the same few people end
up doing all the work.
Contact details are scattered in phones and old spreadsheets, and are lost when
people change numbers.
Mulembe18 solves this by giving the community one trusted home online.
3. Vision, mission and values
Vision: a connected community in Mombasa where nobody who belongs is left out.
Mission: register community members living in Mombasa, bring them together, and make
community events easy to plan and join.

5. Who the site is for
Members: anyone from the community living in Mombasa who wants to be known and
reachable.
Newcomers: students, new workers and families who have just arrived and want to
find their people.
Organisers and leaders: the people who plan gatherings, collect contributions and
share news.
Administrators: a small trusted team that approves members and keeps the platform
in order.
6. What the website will do
6.1 Phase 1 features (the minimum to launch)
Home page. A clear welcome, what Mulembe18 is, a button to register, and the next
upcoming events.

Member registration. A short form collecting only what is needed:
Full name
Phone number (and email if they have one)
Area or estate in Mombasa
Occupation or institution (optional)
How they would like to be contacted
Consent to be listed, with a choice of what is visible
Approval. New registrations are reviewed by an admin before they appear publicly, so the

Member directory. Approved members can search by name or area. Contact details are
only shown to logged-in members, and each person controls what others can see.
Events. A page listing upcoming and past events with date, time, venue, description and
an RSVP button. Organisers can create and edit events. Admins can approve them.
Announcements. A simple notice board for community news, such as bereavements,
celebrations, meetings and opportunities.
Admin dashboard. Approve members, manage events and announcements, and export
the register.
6.2 Phase 2 features (after launch, once people are using it)
SMS and email notifications for new events and reminders.
Event check-in on the day, so attendance is recorded.
Optional contributions for events and welfare, paid by M-Pesa.
Interest groups, for example youth, professionals, students, women, and men.
A photo gallery from past events.
A simple map or list view of members by area.
6.3 Later ideas
A community business directory, so members can support each other's businesses.
A jobs and opportunities board.
A mentorship matching tool for students and young professionals.
Swahili and English language options.

7. Membership, roles and governance
Roles
Visitor: can see the home page, the About page and public events.
Member: an approved person who can see the directory, RSVP to events and post to
the notice board.
Organiser: a member trusted to create events.
Admin: approves members, edits content and sees the full register.
Who runs it. I suggest a small steering group of [3 to 5 people] chosen by the community,
with a chairperson and a secretary. I build and maintain the platform; the steering group
owns the community decisions, such as who may join and what events happen. [Decide
who the first steering group will be.]
Joining rules. Membership is open to community members who live in Mombasa. [Decide
whether a referral from an existing member is required, and how people are verified.]
Conduct. A short code of conduct will be shown at registration: be respectful, no hate
speech, no political campaigning, no selling to members without permission. Admins can
remove members who break it.
8. Privacy and data protection
The site will hold personal information, so this must be handled properly from the start.
Follow the Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019. Check whether the project must register
with the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner, and do it if so.
Collect only what is needed, and say clearly why it is collected.
Ask for consent at registration, and let members edit or delete their profile at any time.
Never show phone numbers or emails to the public, only to logged-in members, and
only if the member allows it.
Store data securely, use HTTPS, protect admin accounts with strong passwords, and
keep backups.
Publish a short, plain-language Privacy Policy and Terms page.

10. Technical approach
I will build the site myself with tools I already work with, keeping it simple and cheap to
run.
Front end: a responsive web app (Next.js with Tailwind CSS), designed mobile first,
because most members will visit on their phones.
Back end and database: a managed database with built-in login (for example Supabase
or Firebase), so I do not have to build authentication from scratch.
Hosting: Vercel or similar, with the custom domain [mulembe18.co.ke or similar, to be
confirmed].
Notifications: Africa's Talking for SMS, and an email service for emails.
Payments (Phase 2): M-Pesa through the Daraja API for event contributions.
Admin tools: a protected admin area inside the same app.
Analytics: a simple, privacy-friendly analytics tool to see visits and registrations.
The home page design is already drafted as a separate design file, and will guide the visual
style: a deep green and amber palette that feels warm and welcoming.

12. Launch and growth plan
Start with trusted people. Ask a few respected members to register first and invite
others.
Go where people already are. Share the registration link in existing WhatsApp groups,
and put a QR code on posters and at events.
Make the first event count. A well-organised first gathering, planned through the site,
is the best advertisement.
Make registering easy. The form should take under two minutes on a phone.
Keep it alive. Post something useful every week: an event, a notice or a welcome to
new members.
Ask for feedback often, and show members what changed because of it.

13. Risks and how to handle them
Few people register. Use trusted leaders to invite others, hold an in-person launch and
keep the form short.
Privacy concerns. Explain clearly how data is used, make visibility optional and never
expose contacts publicly.
Fake or unwelcome sign-ups. Admin approval and, if needed, member referrals.
The site goes quiet. Share the load across a steering group, and schedule regular
content and events.
Running costs. Keep the stack simple and low cost, and use voluntary contributions if
costs grow.
Too much depends on one person. Document how the site works and train a second
admin.
